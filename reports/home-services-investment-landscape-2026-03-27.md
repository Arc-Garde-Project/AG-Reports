### Investment Thesis Themes
1. **Vertical SaaS replacing horizontal tools**: Home services businesses have unique needs (dispatching, job costing, equipment tracking) that Salesforce and QuickBooks can't serve. Purpose-built software wins

4. **Recession-resistant demand**: Emergency HVAC, plumbing, and electrical repairs are nondiscretionary

## 2. Private Equity in Home Services {#2-private-equity}

### The PE Rollup Thesis
Home services is one of the most active PE rollup sectors in North America. The playbook:
1. Acquire a "platform" company at 7-10x EBITDA
2. Execute add-on acquisitions of smaller operators at 4-6x EBITDA
3. Integrate onto a shared tech stack, brand, and back office
4. Sell the consolidated entity at 10-14x EBITDA (multiple arbitrage)

### What Makes a Home Services Tech Company Attractive to PE
Per McKinsey and industry analysis:
- **Recurring revenue** (subscription SaaS, not one-time licenses)
- **High switching costs** (once a contractor's team is trained on your platform, they don't leave)
- **Embedded payments/fintech** (drives usage-based revenue on top of subscriptions)
- **Net Dollar Retention >110%** (customers spend more over time)
- **Fragmented customer base** (no single customer = >5% of revenue)
- **Nondiscretionary demand** (HVAC, plumbing, electrical = recession-resistant)

*Source: [McKinsey - Value Plays in US Home Services](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/private-capital/our-insights/value-plays-in-us-home-services-where-opportunity-meets-reliability)*

### The IAC/Angi Cautionary Tale

The Angi story is the most important cautionary tale in home services tech:

- **2017**: IAC merged HomeAdvisor with Angie's List at ~$500M valuation
- **2018**: Acquired Handy for $165.5M. Rebranded to Angi
- **Peak (2021)**: Market cap in the billions. Revenue growth positive
- **Decline (2022-2025)**: Revenue fell by ~$406M in 2023. Service requests declined 15.8% annually. Network business revenue dropped **79% YoY** in late 2025 due to Google SEO headwinds
- **March 2026**: Market cap collapsed to ~$333M -- roughly what they paid for Angie's List alone
- **April 2025**: IAC completed full spin-off of Angi, making it independent

**Key lesson for investors**: Lead generation marketplace models (pay-per-lead) are vulnerable to platform risk (Google algorithm changes) and have lower defensibility than SaaS models with deep workflow integration.

## 5. Market Gaps Investors Are Looking For {#5-market-gaps}

### What VCs Are Saying (2025-2026)

**The "Unsexy but Massive" Opportunity:**
McKinsey confirms US home services spending is approximately **$700 billion**, with The Home Depot estimating the total addressable market at **$1.1 trillion** ($500B consumer + $600B pro). Yet 80%+ of operators are still small, regional, and founder-led with manual processes. Technology adoption is in early innings.

**The Technology Gap:**
Per McKinsey: "Technology is the core operating system. Too many operators still run on manual processes. Platforms that digitize scheduling, quoting, payments, and CRM see immediate gains in margin and customer satisfaction."

**The Trust/Verification Gap:**
The fragmented, largely offline nature of home services creates major information asymmetry between homeowners and service providers. No dominant platform has solved:
- Real-time contractor verification and credential checking
- Transparent pricing before commitment
- Quality assurance and accountability post-service
- Trust signals that go beyond reviews (which can be faked)

This is an active area of investment interest as the Angi/HomeAdvisor model (pay-per-lead with minimal quality control) has demonstrably failed.

*Source: [McKinsey - Value Plays in US Home Services](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/private-capital/our-insights/value-plays-in-us-home-services-where-opportunity-meets-reliability)*

### AI + Home Services: Emerging Investment Thesis

AI captured **61% of global VC ($258.7B of $427.1B)** in 2025, more than doubling its share since 2022. The intersection with home services is just beginning:

| AI Application | Opportunity | Maturity |
|---------------|-------------|----------|
| **AI scheduling/dispatch optimization** | Reduce drive time 20-30%, increase jobs per day | Early deployments (ServiceTitan adding AI features) |
| **AI-powered pricing/quoting** | Dynamic pricing based on demand, complexity, geography | Pre-product in most cases |
| **Computer vision for diagnostics** | Photo/video-based damage assessment and scoping | R&D stage |
| **AI chatbots for customer intake** | 24/7 booking, triage, and qualification | Entering market |
| **Predictive maintenance** | IoT + AI to predict equipment failure before it happens | IoT infrastructure still building |
| **AI-powered quality assurance** | Automated before/after photo comparison, work verification | Nascent |
| **Humanoid robotics for home tasks** | Household robot market valued at $13.9B (2025), projected $107B by 2034 | Long-term (5-10 years for real utility) |

**VC filter for AI in home services (2026)**: Investors require technical differentiation, market validation through early traction, sound unit economics, and regulatory preparedness. The bar is higher than 2021.

*Sources: [OECD AI VC Report 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/announcements/2026/02/ai-firms-capture-61-percent-of-global-venture-capital-in-2025.html), [Fortune Business Insights - Household Robot Market](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/household-robot-market-113393)*

## 7. TAM/SAM/SOM Methodology for Home Services {#7-tam-sam-som}

### How Successful Home Services Pitches Size the Market

#### TAM (Total Addressable Market)
**Definition**: The entire revenue opportunity if you sold to 100% of the market.

**For home services**:
- US home services spending: **~$700B** (McKinsey estimate)
- The Home Depot estimates total addressable market: **$1.1T** ($500B consumer + $600B pro)
- Global home services market: projected to reach **$1.03T by 2030** from $520B in 2023
- Online/on-demand segment: **$456B (2025)**, growing rapidly

**Best practice**: Use the bottom-up method (MIT's Global Startup Labs recommends counting total customers in market x average annual revenue per customer), not top-down percentage guesses. VCs immediately discount top-down TAM claims.

#### SAM (Serviceable Available Market)
**Definition**: The portion of TAM your product/service can realistically serve given your constraints.

**For home services tech, typical SAM narrows include**:
- Geographic focus (US-only, or specific metros)
- Service vertical (HVAC only, or all trades)
- Business size (5-200 employee contractors, not solo operators)
- Tech-ready segment (businesses willing to adopt software)
- Example: "Of the $700B US market, $250B is in the 5 residential trades we serve (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, landscaping), and $150B of that is served by the 200K+ companies with 5-200 employees that are our ideal customer"

#### SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)
**Definition**: The portion of SAM you can realistically capture in your first 3-5 years.

**For home services tech, SOM typically covers**:
- Specific launch metros (top 20 MSAs)
- Realistic market share (2-5% of SAM in Year 3)
- Must be supported by real acquisition data or bottom-up unit economics
- Example: "We will launch in 10 metros representing 30% of our SAM. With our current CAC of $500 and a sales cycle of 45 days, we project 2,000 customers generating $15M ARR by Year 3"

**Critical warning from investors**: "The market size slide is where most pitch decks lose credibility, with founders presenting huge total addressable markets that they have no realistic path to capturing." Always show the funnel visually: TAM -> SAM -> SOM with clear logic at each step.

*Sources: [Qubit Capital TAM/SAM/SOM Guide](https://qubit.capital/blog/market-size-slide-pitch-deck), [SeedBlink Pitch Guide](https://seedblink.com/blog/2025-02-25-pitch-perfect-how-to-present-tam-sam-som-to-win-over-your-investors), [PitchDrive TAM Guide](https://www.pitchdrive.com/academy/tam-vs-sam-vs-som-market-size-startup), [Hook Agency Home Services Market Size](https://hookagency.com/blog/home-services-market-size-statistics/)*

## 8. Revenue Model Comparisons {#8-revenue-models}

### Model 1: SaaS Subscription (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro)

**How it works**: Monthly/annual subscription fee per user, per technician, or per location.

| Platform | Pricing Structure | Typical Cost | Add-ons |
|----------|------------------|-------------|---------|
| **ServiceTitan** | Per technician, tiered plans | $245-$500+/tech/month | Pro modules add 30-50% to base |
| **ServiceTitan** | Implementation fee | $5,000-$50,000+ | One-time, varies by complexity |
| **Jobber** | Flat monthly fee, tiered | Lower price point than ServiceTitan | Targeting smaller operators |
| **Housecall Pro** | Monthly subscription | Mid-range | Integrated payments |

**ServiceTitan Revenue Breakdown**:
- 71% Platform subscription revenue
- 25% Usage-based FinTech revenue (payments processing)
- 4% Professional services

**Strengths**: Predictable recurring revenue. High switching costs (team training, data lock-in). NDR >110% (customers expand over time). Revenue per customer ~$78K/year.

**Weaknesses**: Longer sales cycles. Higher CAC. Implementation friction. Harder to achieve viral growth.

*Sources: [ServiceTitan Pricing](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing), [MyQuoteIQ ServiceTitan Pricing](https://myquoteiq.com/servicetitan-pricing/), [Sacra ServiceTitan Revenue](https://sacra.com/c/servicetitan/)*

### Model 2: Marketplace Transaction Fee / Pay-Per-Lead (Angi, Thumbtack)

**How it works**: Charge service providers per lead, per quote, or per completed job.

| Platform | Pricing Structure | Typical Cost | Model Details |
|----------|------------------|-------------|--------------|
| **Angi** | Pay-per-lead | $25-$120/lead | Leads auto-assigned to contractors. Negotiable ad pricing |
| **Thumbtack** | Pay-per-quote (credits) | $10-$50/lead | Contractors choose which leads to pursue. More control |

**Strengths**: Lower barrier to entry for providers. Faster onboarding. GMV scales with transaction volume.

**Weaknesses**: **Angi's collapse proves the risk**: revenue declined $406M in a single year. Vulnerable to Google SEO/algorithm changes (79% YoY network revenue drop). Low switching costs for both sides. Providers hate paying for bad leads. No deep workflow integration = no lock-in.

### Model 3: Hybrid (Emerging Best Practice)

**Who does both**: ServiceTitan is the closest example -- SaaS subscription + embedded fintech (payments, financing). Thumbtack is moving toward offering more SaaS-like tools (home management dashboard, project tracking) on top of its marketplace.

**Why hybrid wins**:
- SaaS layer creates switching costs and predictable revenue
- Marketplace/transaction layer captures upside from platform activity
- Embedded fintech adds high-margin revenue without additional CAC
- Data from both sides creates a flywheel (more usage data = better pricing, matching, and predictions)
