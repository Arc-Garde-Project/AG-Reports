## 3. GOOGLE'S PLAY IN HOME SERVICES

### Google Local Services Ads (LSAs)
**Status: DOMINANT and accelerating**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Contractor Adoption (2026) | 70% of contractors in most markets |
| Contractor Adoption (2021) | 28% |
| Cost Per Lead | $20-85 depending on trade/market |
| Lead Cost Increase Since 2023 | 40% in competitive markets |
| Call Increase vs Organic | 25-30% more calls |
| Badge | "Google Verified" (unified Oct 2025) |

**How It Works:**
- Pay-per-lead advertising product for local service businesses
- Ads appear at the VERY TOP of Google search results -- above regular Google Ads, above Map Pack, above organic listings
- Shows business name, star rating, phone number, and trust badge
- Ranking based on: proximity to searcher, review quantity/quality (50+ reviews needed), response time (5 min / 90%+ rate)
- Message leads cost roughly half the price of phone call leads

**Google Verified Badge (formerly Google Guaranteed):**
- Effective October 20, 2025: consolidated "Google Guaranteed," "Google Screened," and "License Verified" into single "Google Verified" badge
- Discontinued the money-back guarantee that came with Google Guaranteed
- Requires: background checks, proof of licensing, insurance documentation
- Process: 2-4 weeks, renewed annually
- Mandatory Google Business Profile verification required since Nov 2024

**Is Google Becoming Dominant?**
YES. Google is the de facto home services marketplace now. Key evidence:
- 70% contractor adoption in 2 years (28% to 70%)
- Leads are EXCLUSIVE (fundamental advantage over Angi/Thumbtack shared leads)
- Top placement in the world's dominant search engine
- 89.62% global search market share as of Jan 2025
- Contractors consistently report higher quality leads than marketplace platforms

**Google's Limitations:**
1. **Category constraints:** Only ~70 service categories supported. No "commercial cleaning" -- forced to use "house cleaning" instead
2. **Limited control:** No targeting customization, ad format control, or placement options
3. **Volume constraints:** Lead volume may be lower than dedicated marketplace platforms
4. **Cost escalation:** 40% increase in competitive markets; climbing fast
5. **Review dependency:** Need 4.8+ star average to compete for top 3 positions; businesses below rarely crack top spots
6. **No relationship layer:** Pure lead generation -- no ongoing homeowner-contractor relationship management
7. **No home management:** Zero features for tracking homes, equipment, maintenance

## 4. AMAZON HOME SERVICES

### Status: EFFECTIVELY DEAD

**Timeline:**
- **2012:** Quiet pilot of home services on small scale
- **2015:** Official national rollout of Amazon Home Services -- furniture assembly, TV mounting, house cleaning, plumbing, even goat rentals for yard grazing
- **2018:** CNN coverage noting mixed results; operational challenges mounting
- **March 2020:** Paused all new service bookings due to COVID-19
- **July 2020:** Permanently discontinued standalone in-home services for third-party sellers
- **2021-present:** Limited to select product-related services (assembly, installation) via subcontractor model only

**Why Amazon Failed:**

1. **Standardization doesn't work for services.** Amazon's core competency is standardizing product delivery. Services are inherently variable -- a house cleaning for a 2,000 sq ft home could take 2 hours or 6 hours depending on condition. Amazon's platform couldn't handle this variability.

2. **Quality control nightmare.** No-shows, incomplete work (workers leaving because "allotted time" expired), bad workmanship. Amazon's reputation for reliability was undermined by service providers they couldn't control.

3. **Provider economics didn't work.** Service providers had to eat losses on mispriced jobs or do redos when customers weren't happy. Amazon's fixed-price model (based on square footage) didn't match real-world labor requirements.

4. **Supply-side acquisition is hard.** Attracting reliable service professionals who are "already overwhelmed by the number of platforms to choose from" proved difficult. Unlike products, you can't warehouse services.

5. **COVID was the final nail.** The pandemic pause gave Amazon a convenient exit from a struggling business. They never reopened it fully.

**Lessons for Our Build:**
- Fixed pricing without understanding job scope = disaster
- You cannot apply product marketplace logic to service marketplaces
- Service quality depends on the provider, not the platform -- trust and vetting matter more than logistics
- Even Amazon's distribution advantage and brand trust couldn't overcome these fundamental challenges
- The relationship between homeowner and service provider is the product; a transaction layer alone fails

## 6. HOME MANAGEMENT STARTUPS

### Why Has Nobody Won This Space?

The home management space has seen multiple entrants and multiple failures. The fundamental challenges:

1. **No willingness to pay.** Homeowners don't perceive home management as a paid utility. Subscription models (Centriq tried this) alienate users.
2. **Data entry burden.** Asking homeowners to manually input all their appliances, model numbers, and maintenance history creates massive friction.
3. **Low engagement frequency.** Unlike social media or messaging, home management is episodic -- maybe monthly. Hard to build habit.
4. **No clear monetization path.** If homeowners won't pay, you need advertising or contractor referral revenue -- which pivots you into a marketplace, competing with giants.
5. **Fragmented use case.** Users need different things: some want inventory tracking, some want maintenance reminders, some want contractor management. Trying to serve all creates complexity.
6. **42% of startups fail due to lack of market need.** The "home management app" category may not have enough standalone demand.

### THE GAP NOBODY HAS FILLED

No platform combines all three of:
1. **Contractor marketplace** (finding and hiring)
2. **Home management** (tracking equipment, maintenance, history)
3. **Ongoing relationship** (contractor-homeowner connection beyond one transaction)

Thumbtack is the closest -- they're adding home management features and have the marketplace. But their core is still shared leads, their home management is new and unproven, and they don't own the ongoing relationship.

The home management apps (Centriq dead, HomeZada tiny, Dib new, DomiDocs niche) have no marketplace.

The marketplaces (Angi, Google LSAs, Yelp) have no home management.

The field service tools (ServiceTitan, Jobber) own the contractor's backend but don't touch the homeowner.

**This gap is the opportunity.**
