## 1. RepeatMD Deep Dive

### Company Profile
- **Founded:** 2021, Houston, TX
- **Revenue (2025):** $13.1M
- **Employees:** 119
- **Category:** AI-Powered Rewards & Ecommerce for Aesthetic and Wellness Practices

### Exact Features
- **MedCommerce Engine:** Patients purchase treatments and memberships 24/7 (86% of in-app purchases happen outside business hours)
- **Beauty Bank:** Digital wallet where patients pre-save funds for future treatments
- **Custom-Branded Mobile App:** Each practice gets a white-labeled app with rewards, ecommerce, memberships
- **Patient Financing:** Affirm integration with instant approval and soft credit checks
- **Memberships:** Recurring monthly revenue; members spend 44% more per year than non-members
- **Automated Referrals & Personalization:** V3 uses AI to drive patient engagement automatically
- **Business Intelligence Dashboard:** Practice analytics and patient behavior data

### Pricing
- **Not transparent.** Multiple reviewers flag pricing as confusing, expensive, and opaque. Several feel cost is excessive; others say justified by features. No public pricing page found.

### $50M Series A (November 2023)
- **Total Raise:** $50M ($40M equity + $10M debt facility from Silicon Valley Bank)
- **Co-Led By:** Centana Growth Partners and Full In Partners
- **Participation:** PROOF, Mercury Fund (also led seed round 9 months prior)
- **The Pitch:** "Automating revenue generation in the aesthetics & wellness industry" -- inbound revenue platform that makes practices sell more without manual effort
- **R&D Investment:** $20M invested in building V3

### Practice & User Metrics
- **Practices:** "Thousands" -- the 2,500 figure and 700K user figure are referenced in earlier marketing but exact current numbers aren't publicly verified
- **Transaction Milestone:** $100M+ in transactions processed through the app
- **Retention Rate:** Not publicly disclosed. Reviews indicate strong retention where practices are engaged, but churn complaints exist around pricing dissatisfaction
- **Engagement Metrics:** Not publicly disclosed beyond the 86% off-hours purchase stat and 44% higher spend for members

### Could They Expand to Trades?
- **No public signals.** Zero mention of home services, trades, HVAC, plumbing, or general contracting in any press release, blog, or interview
- **Their DNA is medical aesthetics:** Botox, fillers, laser treatments, body contouring. The entire product is built around treatment catalogs, patient financing for elective procedures, and before/after tracking
- **International expansion first:** They expanded to Canada (May 2025) before considering any vertical expansion

### What Would Stop Them?
1. **Completely different purchase psychology.** Aesthetics = recurring vanity/wellness purchases. Trades = emergency/necessity purchases. Their gamification model (Beauty Bank, points) doesn't translate
2. **Ticket structure mismatch.** Med spa treatments are $200-$2,000 with high frequency. HVAC/plumbing is $5,000-$15,000 with very low frequency
3. **No contractor-side infrastructure.** Their platform serves practices, not independent contractors. Building a contractor management layer would be a different product
4. **They would have to rebuild from scratch.** Equipment tracking, permit databases, DIY guidance, multi-trade marketplaces -- none of this exists in their stack

**Verdict:** RepeatMD is not a threat to the trades space. Their model is purpose-built for aesthetics and would require a completely new product to enter home services.

## 2. POS-Integrated Loyalty (Square, Toast, Clover)

### Square Loyalty
- **Pricing:** Starts at $45/month, scales with loyalty visit volume per location
- **How it Works:** Card-linked loyalty built into Square POS. Customers enter phone number to enroll. Earn points by visits, amount spent, or items purchased. Rewards = free items, percentage discounts, or category discounts
- **Results:** 26% increase in customer visit frequency
- **Apple Wallet integration** for tracking points
- **30-day free trial**, month-to-month billing

### Toast Loyalty
- **Restaurant-only.** Deep integration with Toast POS, online ordering, and kitchen display
- **Points by spend or visits.** Auto-enrollment when paying with credit card
- **SMS/email marketing built in.** Targeted offers to loyalty members
- **Hard lock-in:** Only works with Toast POS. Cannot be used outside restaurant ecosystem

### Clover Loyalty (Clover Rewards)
- **Built into Clover POS software plans.** Point-per-dollar, item-based perks, or visit milestones
- **Mobile-friendly sign-up.** Every receipt prompts enrollment
- **Customizable reward structures**

### Could Square Build Loyalty for Trade Businesses?
- **Square already serves trades.** Square has a dedicated "Home and Repair Services" product page offering invoicing, payments, scheduling, and customer management for HVAC, plumbing, cleaning, landscaping, and automotive
- **Processing rates:** 2.6% + 15 cents in-person, 3.3% + 30 cents for invoices
- **Square Loyalty is technically available** to any Square merchant, including trade businesses
- **BUT:** No trade business currently uses it as a retention tool. The loyalty model (earn points per visit/spend) doesn't fit trades because visit frequency is too low (1-2x/year). You'd earn 50 points over 10 years -- not engaging
- **The gap:** Square has the payment rails and the contractor relationships but has not built a loyalty model designed for low-frequency, high-ticket service businesses

### Do Any Trade Businesses Use Square + Loyalty?
- No evidence found of any HVAC, plumbing, or electrical contractor running Square Loyalty. The feature is used overwhelmingly by food/beverage, retail, and beauty businesses

**Verdict:** Square has the infrastructure and the contractor relationships to theoretically build this, but their loyalty model is fundamentally designed for high-frequency retail. They'd need a completely different loyalty architecture for trades.

## 3. Belly (Acquired by Mobivity)

### Timeline
- **Founded:** 2011, Chicago. Tablet-based loyalty for brick-and-mortar
- **Peak:** Raised $16.5M+, had 7,000+ merchant locations, 7M+ loyalty members
- **Renamed to Hatch Loyalty**
- **Acquired by Mobivity (MFON):** November 2018 for $3M ($2M cash + $1M debt)
- **Post-acquisition expected value:** $2M annual revenue, 2,000+ merchant locations

### What Happened After Acquisition
- **Mobivity integrated Belly into its "re:currency" platform** (transaction data analytics)
- **Mobivity's stock collapsed:** From ~$2.50 in 2018 to $0.09 (all-time low, September 2024). Currently ~$0.25
- **Mobivity 2024 revenue:** Just $1.14M with losses of -$10.23M
- **The Belly brand technically still exists** on review sites and app stores but is effectively a zombie product
- **Reports of service cessation** without notice, merchants unable to access customer point totals
- **Market cap:** $18.8M (micro-cap, effectively non-operational at scale)

### Why Belly Failed as a Standalone
1. **Hardware dependency.** Tablet-based check-in system required dedicated hardware at the point of sale -- expensive and clunky
2. **No transaction integration.** Belly was a standalone loyalty layer, disconnected from actual payment processing. Winners (Starbucks, Ritual, Square) bundled loyalty + payment + ordering
3. **The loyalty-only model doesn't work.** As the post-mortem analysis revealed: the winning model bundles pre-ordering, payment, and loyalty together, solving real friction (waiting in line) not just gamification (earn points)
4. **No moat.** Any POS with built-in loyalty (Square, Toast, Clover) made Belly redundant

### Lessons for Us
- **Standalone loyalty without embedded utility fails.** Belly proved that points-for-visits alone isn't enough value
- **The bundle wins.** Payment + loyalty + utility = stickiness. Equipment tracking + AI + contractor marketplace + loyalty is a valid bundle
- **Hardware lock-in is deadly.** Everything must be mobile-first, no dedicated hardware
- **Transaction integration is non-negotiable.** If you don't touch the payment, you don't own the relationship

## 5. Automotive Repair Loyalty Programs

### Why Auto Repair Matters
Auto repair is the closest analog to home trades:
- High-ticket ($500-$5,000+ per visit)
- Low-frequency (2-4x per year)
- Trust-dependent (you need to trust your mechanic)
- Emergency-driven (breakdowns aren't planned)
- Relationship-based (people want "their" mechanic)

### Key Players

**BayIQ (Acquired by AutoVitals)**
- Marketing + loyalty software specifically for tire and auto repair shops
- Points-per-dollar-spent model with customizable conversion rates
- Personalized offers based on service history
- Automated email/SMS campaigns based on customer behavior
- Integrates with POS systems
- **Acquired by AutoVitals** -- now part of a broader auto shop management suite

**AutoFlow Rewards & Referrals**
- End-to-end CRM for automotive repair shops
- Built-in rewards and referral system
- Customizable loyalty program within shop management software

**LoyaltyGator (Automotive Vertical)**
- White-label loyalty platform with an automotive vertical
- Points, referrals, and tiered rewards

### What Works in Auto Repair Loyalty
- **Service history-based reminders** (your oil change is due, your brake pads need replacement) -- this is the killer feature
- **Points per dollar spent** with redemption on future services
- **Automated follow-up campaigns** based on when services were last performed
- **Integration with shop management/POS** is essential

### What Doesn't Work
- **Generic punch cards** (5 oil changes = 1 free) have limited impact on high-ticket services
- **Standalone loyalty apps** that aren't integrated with the shop's workflow get ignored
- **One-size-fits-all programs** that don't account for service type differences

### Lessons for Home Services
- Auto repair loyalty succeeds when tied to **predictive maintenance reminders** -- exactly what equipment tracking would do for homeowners
- The **service history = retention trigger** model is directly applicable to HVAC, plumbing, electrical
- Nobody in auto repair has built a **consumer-facing marketplace** with loyalty -- it's all B2B tools for shops

## 6. Home Warranty Companies as Indirect Competitors

### Market Size
- **US market valued at $4.6B-$9.5B** (2025, depending on measurement)
- **Growing at ~8-10% CAGR** through 2032-2035
- **Major players:** American Home Shield, Choice Home Warranty, First American, Cinch Home Services

### Satisfaction Rates
- **80% report satisfied/very satisfied** with plans (industry-reported)
- **But only 49% satisfied with most recent claim outcome** (independent survey)
- **50% experienced poor communication or repair delays**
- **17%+ of claims faced denial or delay** in 2024
- **19,000+ formal disputes** recorded by regulatory agencies across North America
- **36% of online reviews mention technicians refusing repairs after deductibles paid**

### Digital Capabilities

**American Home Shield:**
- Mobile app for making/tracking service requests
- Video chat with repair experts (select plans)
- Member discounts on smart home devices, carpet cleaning, HVAC tune-ups
- $25 Amazon gift card referral program
- **No equipment tracking, no contractor marketplace, no loyalty points**

**Cinch Home Services:**
- Online portal ("My Account") for claims and plan management
- On-Demand Platform for same-day appointments
- $20/month tech support subscription through support.com
- CinchPro app for contractors
- **No equipment tracking beyond warranty claims, no loyalty program**

**First American Home Warranty:**
- Recommends third-party home maintenance apps to customers
- **Does not offer its own equipment tracking or loyalty features**

### Could They Add Loyalty/Rewards/Equipment Tracking?
**Theoretically yes. They have:**
- Existing homeowner relationships (millions of subscribers)
- Contractor networks (thousands of vetted service providers)
- Service history data (every claim = a record)
- Monthly subscription billing relationship

### Why Haven't They?
1. **Their business model profits from NOT paying claims.** Every denial = revenue retained. A loyalty program that tracks equipment and predicts failures would surface more claims, hurting margins
2. **Adversarial relationship with customers.** 50% experience poor communication or delays. Customer satisfaction is structurally low. You can't build loyalty on top of a broken trust foundation
3. **Legacy tech debt.** These are old-line businesses (AHS founded 1971). Digital transformation is slow
4. **No incentive to innovate.** Market is growing 8-10% CAGR with no disruption pressure. Real estate agents bundle warranties into closings -- captive distribution channel
5. **Contractor network is adversarial too.** Contractors hate warranty companies because they're forced to accept low pay and deal with claim bureaucracy. Building a "loyalty" program on a network that resents you is impossible
6. **They would cannibalize themselves.** Good equipment tracking + predictive maintenance = fewer emergency breakdowns = fewer high-margin emergency claims

**Verdict:** Home warranty companies are structurally incapable of building what we're describing. Their entire business model conflicts with proactive maintenance and homeowner empowerment. They are ripe for disruption, not competitors.

## 7. Insurance Companies as Potential Competitors

### Hippo Insurance (Smart Home Leader)
- **Still active and growing.** Provides smart home monitoring devices (SimpliSafe, Ring sensors) free with policies in eligible states
- **Sensors detect:** Smoke/CO, water leaks, motion
- **ADT Partnership:** Free ADT smart home security installation for new customers in select states
- **Hippo Home App:** Personalized maintenance checklists with due dates and automated reminders
- **Approach:** Proactive -- identify small issues before they become big claims

### Could an Insurer Build What We're Building?
**Hippo is the closest insurance company to our model, and they still fall short:**
- They track home *hazards* (leaks, smoke), not *equipment lifecycle*
- No contractor marketplace -- they process claims, not maintenance
- No loyalty/rewards for homeowners
- No DIY guidance or permit info
- Their app is insurance-centric, not homeowner-empowerment-centric

### Why Insurers Won't Build This
1. **Regulatory constraints.** Insurance companies are heavily regulated. Adding contractor marketplaces, loyalty programs, and equipment tracking creates regulatory complexity across 50 states
2. **Claims-avoidance DNA.** Insurers want to prevent claims, not facilitate maintenance services. Building a contractor marketplace creates liability
3. **No contractor relationships.** Insurers contract with adjusters and restoration companies, not HVAC techs and plumbers
4. **Data privacy concerns.** Equipment tracking data used by an insurer could be used to deny claims ("you didn't maintain your furnace as recommended"). Homeowners would never trust it
5. **Conflict of interest.** If your insurer tells you your water heater needs replacing, is that a helpful recommendation or a liability dodge?

**Verdict:** Insurance companies (including Hippo) are building adjacent features but are structurally prevented from building the full vision by regulatory constraints, trust issues, and conflicting incentives.

## 8. Real Estate Tech Companies

### Zillow
- **No post-purchase homeowner engagement tools.** Zillow is pre-purchase (search, Zestimate, mortgage)
- **Shut down Zillow Offers (iBuying)** -- partnered with Opendoor instead
- **No contractor network.** No maintenance features. No loyalty program
- **Zillow Premier Agent** connects buyers with agents, not homeowners with contractors

### Redfin
- **Pre-purchase only.** Search, agent matching, market data
- **Partnership with Zillow** on new construction listings
- **No post-purchase tools, no maintenance, no contractor network**

### Opendoor
- **iBuying + basic ancillary services** (home repairs before resale, mortgage, title)
- **Home repairs are seller-facing** (prepping homes for sale), not homeowner maintenance
- **No homeowner engagement post-purchase**

### Thumbtack (Most Relevant)
- **300,000+ service providers** across 500+ categories
- **OpenAI/ChatGPT Partnership (October 2025):** Thumbtack is the sole home services partner in ChatGPT. Users can ask home questions and get matched with Thumbtack pros without leaving ChatGPT
- **HomeLedger Partnership (September 2025):** HomeLedger users can access Thumbtack's contractor network directly in-app
- **But:** Thumbtack is a lead-gen marketplace, not a loyalty platform. No equipment tracking, no homeowner retention, no rewards. Contractors pay for leads; homeowners get one-off matches

### Porch
- **2.8M professionals** in network, 100+ markets
- **Home Concierge:** Real humans who help with home to-do lists (finding movers, setting up appointments, comparing insurance)
- **90-day quality warranty** on managed work
- **Pivoted to insurance:** Porch is now primarily a home insurance company, not a contractor marketplace
- **No loyalty program, no equipment tracking, no rewards**

**Verdict:** Real estate tech companies abandon homeowners the moment the transaction closes. Thumbtack comes closest to contractor matching but has zero retention/loyalty features. The post-purchase homeowner relationship is an unowned space.

## 11. Verdict: The Gap Is Real

### Nobody Is Building Exactly What We're Describing

After exhaustive search across:
- US startup databases (Crunchbase, AngelList, PitchBook, CB Insights)
- Accelerator portfolios (YC, TechStars, 500 Startups)
- Product Hunt launches (2023-2025)
- International markets (UK, Australia, Europe, Asia)
- Adjacent industry leaders (aesthetics, auto repair, insurance, real estate, retail, POS)
- Fortune 500 corporate initiatives (Lowe's, Home Depot, Zillow, Thumbtack)

**No single entity combines all six pillars:**
1. Verified contractor marketplace
2. Homeowner equipment tracking with lifecycle prediction
3. AI assistant for diagnosis and guidance
4. Loyalty/rewards program for homeowner retention
5. DIY guidance and tutorials
6. Permit information and compliance

### What Exists Is Fragmented

| Pillar | Who Does It | Gap |
|--------|------------|-----|
| Contractor marketplace | Thumbtack, Angi, Porch, AllBetter | No loyalty. Lead-gen only. No homeowner retention |
| Equipment tracking | Oply, HomeZada, HomeLedger, Homer | No contractor marketplace. No loyalty. Standalone utility apps |
| AI assistant | Thumbtack/ChatGPT, Oply, HomeZada, HomeLedger | None integrated with loyalty or contractor marketplace |
| Loyalty/rewards | Lowe's (retail only), RepeatMD (aesthetics only) | Nobody doing loyalty for home services trades |
| DIY guidance | YouTube, HomeZada, HomeKeep | Not integrated with equipment tracking or contractor fallback |
| Permit info | Nobody | Complete white space |

### The Structural Reason Nobody Has Built This

1. **Two-sided marketplace is hard.** You need contractors AND homeowners. Most startups pick one side
2. **Loyalty requires frequency.** Traditional loyalty models need frequent transactions. Home services are 1-3x/year. Nobody has designed a loyalty model for low-frequency, high-trust services
3. **Equipment tracking requires data.** You need appliance databases, lifecycle models, maintenance schedules. This is a hard data problem
4. **The adjacent players can't pivot.** RepeatMD is locked into aesthetics. Square's loyalty model doesn't fit. Warranty companies profit from the opposite incentive. Insurers face regulatory barriers
5. **The big tech players don't care enough.** Zillow, Redfin, and Opendoor make money on transactions, not relationships. Thumbtack makes money on leads, not retention
6. **Lowe's is the only real threat** but they're a retailer, not a platform. Their incentive is selling products, not empowering homeowners

### Proof of the Gap

- $317M paid for FiveStars (loyalty for SMBs) -- but nobody has built loyalty for trades
- $50M raised by RepeatMD (loyalty for aesthetics) -- but nobody has applied this model to home services
- Lowe's investing heavily in Digital Home Platform -- but limited to their product ecosystem
- Thumbtack partnered with OpenAI -- but still no homeowner retention or loyalty features
- Home warranty industry is $4.6B-$9.5B -- with 50% customer dissatisfaction and zero innovation in loyalty
- Home services market projected to grow by $6.5B through 2028 (40% CAGR)

### The Opportunity Window

**Right now, in March 2026:**
- Oply has $180K and the right idea but wrong execution and no loyalty
- HomeLedger has $1.5M and Thumbtack integration but no loyalty
- Lowe's has HomeCare+ but is limited to their ecosystem
- Nobody has loyalty/rewards for trades
- Nobody has permit info
- Nobody has combined all six pillars

**The window is open. Nobody is coming from the adjacent industries because their business models structurally prevent it. The startups in the space are underfunded and incomplete. The corporate players are trapped by their incentives.**
