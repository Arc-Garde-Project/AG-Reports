## 1. FTC Actions

### FTC Action #1: Deceptive Lead Marketing ($7.2M -- 2023)

**What happened:** In March 2022, the FTC filed an administrative complaint against HomeAdvisor, Inc. (doing business as Angi Leads). In January 2023, the FTC issued a consent order requiring HomeAdvisor to pay up to **$7.2 million**. The final order was approved in **April 2023**.

**What they were caught doing:**

- **Lying about lead quality:** HomeAdvisor claimed service providers would get leads matching their skills and preferred locale. The FTC found many leads did not match.
- **Fabricating conversion rates:** HomeAdvisor told service providers their leads result in jobs at rates "much higher than the company could substantiate."
- **Hiding fees as free offers:** HomeAdvisor misrepresented that the optional one-month mHelpDesk subscription was "free" when it actually cost $59.99.
- **Misleading about lead sources:** HomeAdvisor represented most leads came from their website, when a majority were actually from third-party affiliates (lower quality).

**These practices had been ongoing since at least mid-2014** -- nearly a decade of deception.

**Refund details:**
- $3 million returned to **110,372 businesses** that paid for HomeAdvisor memberships
- 91,273 businesses eligible to claim an additional $59.99 each for the mHelpDesk deception
- Average refund per affected business: approximately **$27** (on memberships costing $287.99/year)

**Source:** [FTC Press Release - Jan 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/01/ftc-order-requires-homeadvisor-pay-72-million-stop-deceptively-marketing-its-leads-home-improvement) | [FTC Final Order - Apr 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/04/ftc-approves-final-order-against-homeadvisor-inc-deceptively-marketing-its-leads-home-improvement) | [FTC Case File](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/1923106-homeadvisor-matter)

### FTC Action #2: Handy Technologies / Misleading Worker Wages ($2.95M -- 2025)

**What happened:** In January 2025, the FTC and New York Attorney General Letitia James secured **$2.95 million** from Angi Services (operator of Handy Technologies).

**What they were caught doing:**

- **Inflating advertised wages:** Ran tens of thousands of ads across New York inflating hourly rates. Workers were paid **nearly 50% less** than the advertised rate in some cases.
- **Lying about payment timing:** Ads stated workers would be paid daily; in reality, workers were paid nearly a week later unless they paid a fee.
- **Hidden fines and punitive protocols:** Workers were fined $50 when a customer failed to provide job site access. To avoid the fine, workers had to enable GPS tracking, check in through the app, attempt to reach the customer, and wait at the job site for 30 minutes.

**Source:** [NY AG Press Release](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2025/attorney-general-james-and-ftc-secure-295-million-angi-services-misleading) | [FTC Press Release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-new-york-attorney-general-take-action-against-handy-technologies-deceiving-workers-about)

### FTC Enforcement Summary

## 2. State Attorney General Actions

### Vermont AG -- "Certified Pro" Deception ($100K -- Oct 2025)

**What happened:** Attorney General Charity Clark settled with Angi over misleading marketing.

**What they were caught doing:**

- Angi advertised Vermont contractors as **"Angi Certified Pros"**
- Vermont does not have a "certification" process for contractors -- only registration
- **Angi itself has no certification process** and cannot confer or vouch for credentials
- The term "Certified Pro" implied a governmental credential that did not exist

**Settlement terms:**
- Stop using "Angi Certified Pro" and any term implying governmental credential
- Direct users to state resources to verify contractor credentials
- Contact all Vermont residential contractors about their registration obligations
- Pay **$100,000** to the State of Vermont

**Source:** [Vermont AG Press Release](https://ago.vermont.gov/blog/2025/10/13/attorney-general-clark-settles-dispute-angi-over-misleading-marketing-practice) | [Vermont Daily Chronicle](https://vermontdailychronicle.com/vermont-ag-halts-angis-certified-pro-deception/) | [Compass Vermont Analysis](https://www.compassvermont.com/p/vermont-ag-halts-angis-certified)

- **Filed:** July 16, 2018 in U.S. District Court, Denver
- **Plaintiffs:** 880+ home service professionals spanning several states
- **Core allegation:** Customer leads were **"overwhelmingly bogus"**
- **Specific charges:**
  - Leads "over distributed" (sent to too many contractors simultaneously)

## 6. The Angie's List to Angi Merger Disaster

### What Angie's List Was Before

- Founded 1995 as a **trusted, review-based platform**
- **Subscription model:** Homeowners paid to access verified contractor reviews
- Reviews were from **verified, paying members** -- not anonymous internet users
- Contractors couldn't pay to manipulate rankings
- Built its reputation on **trust and independence**

### The Merger

- **May 2017:** IAC (owner of HomeAdvisor) acquired Angie's List for **$8.50/share (~$500M)**
- **September 29, 2017:** Merger completed, forming ANGI Homeservices
- **October 2, 2017:** Trading begins on NASDAQ under "ANGI"
- **March 2021:** Rebranded from "ANGI Homeservices" to **"Angi Inc."**

### How HomeAdvisor's Lead-Gen Model Destroyed Trust

HomeAdvisor's business model was fundamentally different from Angie's List:

| | Angie's List (Before) | Angi (After) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue model | Homeowner subscriptions | Contractor lead fees |
| Reviews | Verified members only | Open/unverified |
| Contractor ranking | Merit-based | **Pay-to-play** |
| Lead exclusivity | N/A | Shared with 3-8 competitors |
| Verification | Reputation-based | Self-reported |
| Trust source | Consumer advocacy | Advertising revenue |

The merger took a **consumer trust brand** and bolted a **pay-to-play lead mill** on top of it.

## 7. Fake/Misleading Verification

### What "Angi Certified" Actually Verifies

**Almost nothing.** Here is what Angi checks vs. what they imply:

| Verification Step | What Angi Claims | What Actually Happens |
|---|---|---|
| Licensing | Implied by "Certified" label | **Self-reported.** Contractors check a box. Angi does not verify against state databases. |
| Insurance | Implied | **Self-reported.** Angi "strongly encourages" homeowners to verify independently. |
| Background checks | "Certified" contractors have background checks | Only business **owners** -- not employees who enter your home. Only within last 2 years. Not for all designation types. |
| Credentials | "Certified Pro" implies government credential | **No certification process exists.** Vermont AG confirmed Angi "cannot confer or vouch for credentials." |
| Quality of work | Implied by "Certified" branding | **No work quality verification.** No reference checks. No job inspections. |

- Angi relies on businesses to **"self-report"** their licensing

- The project manager admitted on the phone he was unlicensed and "didn't know he needed one"
- The contractor received only a **$500 civil penalty** from the state

## 8. Contractor Exodus

### Why Contractors Are Leaving

1. **Shared leads** -- sold to 3-8 competitors simultaneously
2. **Rising costs** -- $40-$200+ per lead
3. **Low close rates** -- 5-15% (vs. Angi's inflated claims the FTC flagged)
4. **No exclusivity** -- you're racing other contractors for every lead
5. **Refund difficulty** -- near impossible to get credits for bad leads
6. **Contract traps** -- 12-month auto-renewing contracts, 30-35% early cancellation penalty, 60 days notice required

### The Math Doesn't Work

- Contractors report spending **$3,000-$5,000/month** with only **2-3 jobs** to show for it
- Effective customer acquisition cost: **$1,400+ per booked job**
- On a $10,000 remodeling job, that's **14% of revenue** just for lead acquisition
- This is **4-5x the cost** of running your own Google Ads or SEO

### What They Switch To

1. **Google Ads / Local Services Ads** -- lower cost, exclusive leads
2. **SEO / own website** -- best long-term ROI
3. **Thumbtack** -- cheaper per lead, more transparent
4. **Facebook Ads** -- direct targeting, no middleman
5. **Word of mouth / referrals** -- zero acquisition cost

## 9. Homeowner Horror Stories

Angi's "Happiness Guarantee" is marketed as consumer protection, but:

- Multiple documented cases of claims being denied

The fundamental problem: Angi profits from **selling leads to contractors**, not from ensuring homeowners get quality work. Once the lead is sold and the contractor is billed, Angi has no financial incentive to ensure the job goes well.

## Key Takeaways for Competitive Positioning

1. **Angi is a government-sanctioned fraudster.** Two separate FTC actions totaling $10.15M in penalties. This isn't opinion -- it's federal record.
