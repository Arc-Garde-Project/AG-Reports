# Competitive Landscape: Home Services Platforms
**Prepared for Business Proposal | March 27, 2026**
**AG Research Report**

---

## Executive Summary

The home services industry is a $657B+ total addressable market growing 7-10% annually. Current platforms fall into two categories: (1) lead generation marketplaces (Angi, Thumbtack) that connect homeowners with contractors but are widely distrusted by both sides, and (2) operations/FSM software (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, FieldEdge) that help contractors run their businesses but have zero consumer-facing loyalty or trust features. No platform currently combines verified expert matching, homeowner equipment tracking, AI assistance, rewards/loyalty, DIY guidance, and permit verification into a single consumer-facing experience.

## 1. Angi (formerly HomeAdvisor + Angie's List)

### Company Overview
- **Ticker:** ANGI (NASDAQ) / ANGIV post-restructuring
- **Market Cap:** ~$302-333M (as of March 2026)
- **FY 2025 Revenue:** $1.03B (down 13% from $1.185B in FY 2024)
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** $140M (2025); guided $145-150M for 2026
- **2026 Revenue Outlook:** Low single-digit growth (1-3%)
- **Employees:** ~2,800 (before Jan 2026 layoffs of 350)

### Platform Scale
- **Service Professionals:** ~134,000 active pros (early 2025)
- **Homeowners Served:** 150M+ people served historically; 300M+ projects facilitated (cumulative, not active users)
- **Total Addressable Market:** $657B per Angi's own research

### Revenue Model (How They Make Money)
1. **Pay-Per-Lead (Angi Leads):** Contractors pay $15-$85+ per lead depending on trade and location. HVAC leads run $65-85 in major metros; roofing pushes $100+. Leads are shared with 3-8 contractors simultaneously
2. **Advertising (Angi Ads):** Contractors pay for enhanced visibility, higher search rankings on the platform. Pro subscriptions start at ~$200/month
3. **Annual Membership:** Contractors pay ~$300/year to access Angi Leads
4. **Homeowner Subscription:** Angi Key membership at $29.99/year gives 20% discount on booked services
5. **Contract Lock-in:** 12-month contracts with auto-renewal; early termination fees of 30-35% of remaining contract value; 60-day cancellation notice required

### Lead Conversion Rates
- **Angi's Claimed Rate:** ~33% (1 in 3 leads converts to a job)
- **Real-World Contractor Reports:** ~13% average conversion rate on shared leads
- **Exclusive Lead Comparison:** Contractors report 27% conversion on exclusive leads from other sources vs. 13% on Angi shared leads
- **True Cost Per Acquired Customer:** Typically exceeds $1,400 when factoring in the low conversion rate on shared leads

### Major Complaints

**From Contractors:**
- Shared leads (3-8 contractors get the same lead) destroy close rates
- High volume of fake, irrelevant, or unqualified leads
- Leads from people who never requested the service
- Difficulty getting refunds for bad leads
- 12-month lock-in contracts with punitive early termination
- Cost per lead has risen significantly year over year
- FTC ordered $7.2M payment (Jan 2023) for deceptive lead-selling practices; $3M+ in refunds mailed to 110,000+ contractors
- BBB average: 1.96 out of 5 stars across 3,000+ reviews

**From Homeowners:**
- Pay-to-play structure: contractors who pay more rank higher, not those who are better
- "Certified Pro" terminology found misleading (Vermont AG settled for $100K in Oct 2025)
- Reports of contractors not being screened properly
- Scam incidents from unvetted "professionals"
- Ongoing class-action lawsuit over unsolicited text messages (TCPA violations)
- Review integrity questioned due to pay-to-play model

### Recent News (2025-2026)
- **April 2025:** Spun off from IAC; created significant selling pressure on stock
- **January 2026:** Laid off 350 employees (12.5% of workforce), citing AI-driven efficiencies
- **Cost Savings Target:** $70-80M annually from AI restructuring
- **Restructuring Charges:** $22-30M split across Q4 2025 and Q1 2026
- **CEO:** Jeff Kip (took over April 2024, former CFO of IAC)
- **Stock Performance:** Trading $12-15 range (early 2026), off historic lows but well below pandemic peaks. Consensus analyst target: $16-20. Rating: "Hold" / "Moderate Buy"
- **Strategy Shift:** Pivoting to AI, prioritizing proprietary channels (growing high single to low double digits) over third-party channels

## 3. HomeAdvisor (Now Part of Angi) -- Historical Context

### Merger Timeline
- **1998:** Founded as ServiceMagic
- **2012:** Rebranded to HomeAdvisor (widely viewed as an attempt to escape bad reputation)
- **2017:** IAC acquired Angie's List for $500M and merged it with HomeAdvisor
- **2021:** Unified brand became "Angi"
- **Current:** HomeAdvisor brand still exists but is "Powered by Angi"

### Why Contractors Hate the Platform (Specific Complaints)
1. **Lead Reselling:** Same lead sold to 3-8 contractors, creating a race-to-the-bottom on price
2. **Fake Leads:** Contractors report leads from people who never requested service
3. **No Real Screening:** Despite marketing "pre-screened" contractors, Suffolk County NY consumer affairs called Angi "the worst company for home improvements" and said pros "are not screened in any way shape or form"
4. **FTC Enforcement:** $7.2M settlement in January 2023 for "deceptive and misleading tactics in selling leads." Over $3M refunded to 110,000+ contractors. 91,000+ additional claim forms sent
5. **Merger Destroyed Angie's List Trust:** The original Angie's List was a paid, subscriber-based review platform trusted by homeowners. After merging with HomeAdvisor's aggressive lead-gen model, the trust that Angie's List had built over 20+ years was diluted
6. **Contract Traps:** Auto-renewing 12-month contracts, 30-35% early termination fees, aggressive sales tactics

### The Core Problem the Merger Created
Angie's List was built on consumer trust (paid reviews, verified quality). HomeAdvisor was built on lead volume (pay-per-lead, aggressive sales). The merger prioritized the HomeAdvisor revenue model, alienating both the contractors who valued Angie's List's quality reputation and the homeowners who trusted its reviews.

- **IPO Valuation:** ~$9B at open

- **Target Customers:** HVAC, plumbing, electrical, water treatment (76% have 11-200 employees)

### What They Actually Do (Operations, Not Consumer-Facing)
ServiceTitan is a B2B operations platform for trade businesses. It provides:
- Scheduling and dispatching
- CRM and customer management
- Invoicing and payment processing
- Marketing automation
- Financial reporting and accounting
- Mobile app for field technicians
- Sales analytics and pattern analysis

### Why They DON'T Solve the Consumer Trust Problem
1. **Purely B2B:** ServiceTitan sells to contractors, not homeowners. Homeowners never interact with the platform
2. **No Consumer Marketplace:** There is no homeowner-facing component for finding, vetting, or reviewing contractors
3. **No Loyalty/Rewards:** No system for incentivizing repeat homeowner behavior
4. **No Equipment Tracking:** Does not track homeowner equipment, maintenance history, or provide DIY guidance
5. **No Verification Layer:** Does not verify contractor quality from the homeowner's perspective
6. **Operations Focus:** Helps contractors run their businesses efficiently but does nothing to build consumer trust or transparency in the marketplace

ServiceTitan makes contractors more efficient at running operations. It does not make them more trustworthy to homeowners. These are fundamentally different problems.

## 6. RepeatMD

### Company Overview
- **What They Do:** AI-powered loyalty, e-commerce, and patient financing platform for aesthetic and wellness practices (med spas, dermatologists, plastic surgeons, weight loss clinics)
- **Founded:** October 2021 by Phil Sitter
- **Funding:** $50M Series A (November 2023), co-led by Centana Growth Partners and Full In Partners, with participation from PROOF and Mercury Fund. Includes $10M debt facility from Silicon Valley Bank
- **Practices on Platform:** 2,500+ across all 50 states
- **Registered Users:** ~700,000
- **Growth:** +2,519% GMV revenue increase, +130% SaaS revenue increase in 12-month period

### Platform Capabilities
- White-label mobile rewards program
- E-commerce for treatment packages
- Patient financing (partnership with Affirm)
- Loyalty points and retention automation
- "Beauty Bank" savings feature for patients

### Why They Didn't Expand to Trades
1. **Vertical Focus:** Built specifically for aesthetics/wellness -- the buying psychology, pricing models, and repeat visit patterns are completely different from home services
2. **High-Margin Services:** Med spas have 60-80% margins on treatments, making loyalty programs financially viable. Home services margins are much thinner
3. **Consumer-Initiated:** Aesthetic treatments are elective, repeat purchases. Home services are often urgent, one-time needs (broken pipe, roof leak)
4. **No Technical Complexity:** Med spa services don't require permits, equipment tracking, or code compliance
5. **Different Relationship Model:** Med spa patients have ongoing relationships with their provider. Homeowners may not need the same contractor again for years

RepeatMD proves the loyalty model works in services, but the home services vertical has fundamentally different economics and consumer behavior that require a purpose-built solution.

## 7. Loyalty/Rewards Platforms in Home Services

### What Currently Exists

**Retailer Loyalty Programs (Materials, Not Services):**
- **Home Depot Pro Xtra:** Loyalty program for contractors buying materials. Rewards on purchases, not for service quality or homeowner retention
- **Lowe's MVPs Pro Rewards:** Recently expanded (March 2025). Points-based rewards for contractor purchases at Lowe's
- **TimberTech "The Board":** Contractor loyalty for specific product purchases

**B2B Incentive Platforms:**
- **Incentive Group (inGauge):** White-label contractor loyalty programs for manufacturers/distributors to incentivize contractor purchases of their products
- **Lift & Shift:** Distributor-to-contractor reward programs
- **Snipp:** Contractor incentive programs for brands

**Consumer-Facing Home Management Apps (No Loyalty):**
- **HomeZada:** Home inventory, maintenance scheduling, project tracking. No contractor marketplace or rewards
- **Dwellin:** AI-powered home management, task calendars, home inventory. No contractor connection or loyalty
- **Centriq:** Appliance tracking by model number. No services marketplace
- **HomeKeep:** Home maintenance scheduling. No rewards or contractor vetting
- **HomeLedger:** Maintenance tracking app. No loyalty component

**Permit-Focused Startups:**
- **PermitFlow:** $54M Series B (2025). AI-powered construction permitting automation. Shortened timelines by 60%, reduced admin by 90%. But this is B2B for builders/contractors, not consumer-facing
- **Spacial:** $10M funding. Automates permits for affordable home construction
- **CivCheck:** AI for building permit review

### The Critical Finding

**No platform exists that combines all of these for homeowners:**
- Verified expert matching
- Equipment/home tracking
- AI assistance for homeowners
- Loyalty/rewards for repeat use
- DIY guidance
- Permit verification

The existing landscape is completely fragmented:
- Want to find a contractor? Angi/Thumbtack (but trust is broken)
- Want to manage your home? HomeZada/Dwellin (but no contractor connection)
- Want permit help? PermitFlow (but it's for builders, not homeowners)
- Want loyalty rewards? Only exists for material purchases at big box stores
- Want DIY guidance? YouTube (no integration with anything)
- Want contractor operations? ServiceTitan/Jobber (but homeowners never see it)

## 8. The Market Gap

### What Nobody Has Built

There is no single platform that serves as the **homeowner's operating system** -- combining:

| Feature | Who Does Part of It | What's Missing |
|---------|---------------------|----------------|
| Verified expert matching | Angi, Thumbtack | Trust is broken; pay-to-play corrupts rankings |
| Home/equipment tracking | HomeZada, Dwellin, Centriq | No connection to services or contractors |
| AI assistance for homeowners | ChatGPT, YouTube | Not integrated with actual service providers |
| Loyalty/rewards for homeowners | Nobody in home services | Only exists for material purchases (Home Depot, Lowe's) |
| DIY guidance | YouTube, Reddit | Fragmented, no personalization, no equipment context |
| Permit verification | PermitFlow, Spacial | B2B only, not consumer-facing |

### The Trust Crisis

The core problem in home services is **broken trust on both sides:**

**Homeowners don't trust platforms** because:
- Pay-to-play corrupts contractor rankings
- "Verified" and "Certified" labels are marketing, not quality signals
- FTC actions and state AG settlements confirm deceptive practices
- Reviews can be manipulated
- No accountability after the job is done

**Contractors don't trust platforms** because:
- Shared leads destroy their economics ($1,400+ real cost per customer)
- Fake/unqualified leads waste time and money
- Lock-in contracts with punitive termination fees
- Platforms prioritize volume over quality
- No reward for being excellent -- only for paying more

### The $657B Opportunity

- **Total Addressable Market:** $657B (per Angi's own research)
- **US Home Services Market:** $211.71B (2023), projected $893.18B by 2031
- **Online On-Demand Home Services:** Projected $3.64 trillion by 2030
- **Annual Growth Rate:** 7-10%
- **Angi's Revenue (declining):** $1.03B -- proving the market wants something different
- **Angi's Market Cap ($302M) vs TAM ($657B):** The incumbent is valued at 0.046% of the market it serves

### What Would Win

A platform that:
1. **Earns trust through transparency** -- not pay-to-play rankings, but verified performance data
2. **Tracks the homeowner's world** -- their equipment, maintenance history, home systems
3. **Provides AI-powered guidance** -- personalized to their actual home and equipment
4. **Rewards loyalty** -- the first true loyalty/rewards program for homeowners using home services
5. **Connects to verified experts** -- contractors earn their position through quality, not ad spend
6. **Handles permits** -- demystifies the permit process for homeowners
7. **Bridges DIY and professional** -- helps homeowners know what they can do themselves and when to call a pro

This is a greenfield opportunity. The incumbents are either lead-gen marketplaces with broken trust (Angi, Thumbtack) or B2B operations tools that homeowners never see (ServiceTitan, Jobber). Nobody occupies the middle.
